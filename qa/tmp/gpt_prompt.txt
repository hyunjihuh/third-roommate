SCRIPTS
S01: Hi, we are Hyunji Huh, Eunji Shin, and Inseong Seo. We introduce The Third Roommate. We built Bora, an agent that helps roommates handle small requests and permissions.
S02: Our users are university roommates sharing a dorm room. Most clashes are small, like turning on a light while the other sleeps or taking a call at night. Because they're small, they're hard to bring up. People hold back so they don't seem picky, and then holding back looks exactly like being fine.
S03: So we built one agent per room that both roommates share. You can ask agent Bora whether it's okay to do something right now, and Bora decides based on the rules each of you set. If it's already agreed or written down, Bora answers right away. If there's no information, Bora asks your roommate for you.
S04: What the two agree on is stored as cards. For six of the most common dorm conflicts from prior research, each person decides: ask me every time, it's fine so just go ahead, or here's the condition that makes it okay. Your roommate only sees that you decided, not the details, so when they want to know, they ask Bora and Bora answers for you.
S05: Here's the first route. Haneul asks if she can take a call in the room. Bora asks how long. Ten minutes fits Bada's card, under fifteen minutes, so Bora answers right away with the card, and Bada just gets a notification.
S06: When nothing is agreed yet, Bora asks for you. Bora softens the wording but keeps the time, place, and who does what, and only the sentence you approve is sent. If Bada is asleep, the letter waits. In the morning Bada replies, 'I can, with a condition.'
S07: When needs clash, Bora helps them find options together. Haneul wants to tutor in the room at 8 am, but Bada needs to sleep until 9. Bora asks each of them questions fitted to their side, like 'Does it have to be in the room?' for Haneul and 'From what time would it be okay?' for Bada. Then it suggests several options, such as moving to the study room, starting at 9, or using earplugs.
S08: Behind the screen, the work is split into seven model calls. The model only proposes; code rechecks the cards, conditions, and rules; and people make the choice.
S09: Two pairs with different levels of closeness used Bora in their own rooms for three days. We read the app logs and interviewed them before and after. Three things surprised us.
S10: First, the private field became a place to vent. In a private question, P2 wrote 'Honestly, I've held it in for three weeks' and didn't check 'Must include'. P2 said it wasn't meant to be sent, just written somewhere, and later brought it up in the pair interview. P1 hadn't known it bothered P2 that much.
S11: Second, users put their own voice back into Bora's sentences. Asking P4 to set only two alarms, P3 added back '가능하면', 'if possible', and a teary emoticon. P3 said it only felt like P3's own message with both in it, and P4 said it sounded exactly like P3, so P4 cut back right away.
S12: Third, Bora became a mailbox for the two of them. P4 used it to apologize and to invite P3 to a Chiikawa movie, and P3 used the condition button to move it to Sunday. But because the format is a request, the invitation arrived as '부탁해요', a favor, and P4 said it went like handing in homework.
S13: Thank you.
